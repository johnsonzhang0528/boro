    <h2>Understanding Problem Gambling</h2>
    <p>For a small number of people, gambling can cease to be fun and become a problem. Signs of a gambling disorder may include an inability to stop, borrowing money to gamble, or risking family and employment for the sake of gambling.</p>

    <h2>Self-Assessment Test</h2>
    <p>Ask yourself the following questions. If you answer "yes" to several, you may want to consider seeking help.</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Does gambling prevent you from attending work or classes?</li>
      <li>Do you gamble to pass the time or to escape boredom?</li>
      <li>Have others ever criticized you for your gambling?</li>
      <li>Have you lost interest in family, friends, or hobbies due to gambling?</li>
      <li>Have you ever lied to cover up the amount of money or time you spend gambling?</li>
      <li>Are you reluctant to spend "gambling money" on anything else?</li>
      <li>Do you gamble until you lose all your money?</li>
      <li>After losing, do you feel an urgent need to try and win back your losses?</li>
      <li>Do arguments, frustrations, or disappointments make you want to gamble?</li>
      <li>Does gambling make you feel depressed or suicidal?</li>
    </ul>
